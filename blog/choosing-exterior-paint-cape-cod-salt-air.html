How to Choose Exterior Paint That Survives Cape Cod's Salt Air

By Douglas Sartori · August 20, 2026 · Sartori Corporation

A house two miles from the water deals with a different set of conditions than the same house inland. Salt in the air, wind-driven rain, intense summer sun, and New England's swing between humid summers and freezing winters all work against an exterior finish. Choosing the right paint, and the right prep, is what determines whether that finish lasts years or fails early.

Why Coastal Homes Are Harder on Paint

Airborne salt is corrosive and settles on siding, trim, and shingles even well back from the shoreline. Combined with UV exposure and moisture cycling through wood as it expands and contracts, coastal surfaces break down finishes faster than inland homes see. Sun-facing and salt-exposed elevations are almost always the first to show wear.

What to Look for in a Coastal-Grade Exterior Paint

Not every exterior paint is built for this. Look for products with strong UV resistance, flexibility (to move with wood as it expands and contracts without cracking), and genuine resistance to mildew and moisture intrusion. Economy paint lines are formulated to look fine on the shelf and in mild conditions, they're not built for a house that takes wind off the water.

Why We Use Benjamin Moore, Farrow & Ball, and Fine Paints of Europe

We chose these three lines specifically because they hold up to what Cape Cod throws at a house. They cost more per gallon than big-box alternatives, but the depth of color, coverage, and durability translate into a finish that doesn't need attention again in two or three years. We apply three coats as standard practice, not an upsell.

Prep Matters as Much as the Paint Itself

Even the best paint fails on a poorly prepped surface. Our exterior process includes power washing and soft washing (a lower-pressure, chemical-assisted wash used on delicate surfaces like cedar shingles and roofing), scraping, sanding, and priming bare wood before a single finish coat goes on. Where trim or siding has rotted, we replace it, painting over damaged wood only hides the problem.

Cedar Shingles Need a Different Approach

Cape Cod's classic shingle-style homes need a finish, and a prep process, suited to cedar specifically. Cedar breathes and moves differently than clapboard or composite siding, and it's more sensitive to high-pressure washing, which is why soft washing is often the better choice there. Getting this wrong shortens the life of the shingles as well as the finish.
